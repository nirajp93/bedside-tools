Who needs platelet counts on heparin

1 · Mechanism

An antibody turns heparin against platelets

Step through it: each tap is one link in the chain.

2 · Timing

The fall comes on day 5 to 10

The immune response takes days. A fall that starts sooner needs a recent exposure. Pick a pattern.

3 · Pretest probability

The 4Ts score

Tap a score for each item: the band and what it means update below.

4 · The tests

Reading the immunoassay

The immunoassay finds the antibody, but many antibodies cannot activate platelets. The higher the optical density, the likelier a functional assay is positive. Drag it.

5 · Narrowing

Why are the platelets low?

First: a repeat count in a citrate tube if clumping is possible, a smear, PT/INR and fibrinogen. Then, only if the 4Ts is 4 or more: the immunoassay. Tap what you found: causes that no longer fit fade, each with the reason.

6 · Treatment

Treat the clot risk, not the count

Stopping heparin is not enough, and two reflexes make it worse. Tap each.

7 · Afterwards

Acute, subacute, remote

The antibody fades over weeks to months. What is safe depends on the phase. Tap one.

8 · Evidence

The studies behind it

Randomized trials in HIT are few and small; most evidence is cohorts with historical controls.

For education only. Not a substitute for clinical judgment. For adults with suspected heparin-induced thrombocytopenia. Out of scope: pregnancy, children, ECMO and ventricular assist device circuits. Argatroban is labeled in the US for HIT; bivalirudin only for PCI in patients with HIT; fondaparinux and the direct oral anticoagulants are used off-label. Danaparoid, named in the ASH guideline, is not available in the US. Assays and their cutoffs differ by laboratory, and heparin-containing products and infusion concentrations differ by hospital: adjust to your institution, and involve hematology and pharmacy. Evidence tiers are shown as ASH strength and certainty, CHEST grade, the drug label, or "expert practice" for choices made for this tool.
