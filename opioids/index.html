5 · How good are the numbers?

A rough guide, not a law

Equianalgesic ratios come mostly from small single-dose and observational studies, and people vary a lot. No trial has tested one conversion strategy against another, so there are no trial cards here.

That is why the tool always cuts the calculated dose, adds a breakthrough dose, and asks you to reassess within a day.

Equal doses and MME factors

Why: The equal doses are for switching; the MME factors are for judging overdose risk and are not for switching.

More

Equal doses from Fast Fact #36 (4th edition, 2024): morphine 10 mg IV = 25 mg oral = hydromorphone 2 mg IV = 5 mg oral; hydrocodone 25 mg oral = oxycodone 20 mg oral. The oral-to-IV and IV-to-oral ratios may not be the same; this is a rough guide.

MME factors from CDC 2022 (oral and transdermal only; fentanyl per mcg/h). The CDC table says not to use MME to work out the dose when converting one opioid to another. Oxycodone is 1.5 in the CDC table and 1.25 in the conversion table: the CDC number errs high for risk counting, the conversion number errs low for safety when switching.

Not converted by ratio here: oxymorphone, codeine, tramadol, tapentadol, methadone (away from it), the fentanyl patch (away from it) and IV fentanyl (except to and from the patch). Buprenorphine is outside this tool.

Fentanyl patch

Starting a patch

Oral morphine mg/dayPatch mcg/h

Why: The label's table deliberately underestimates, because the patch is slow to reach levels and slow to leave.

More

Only for opioid-tolerant patients (see below). Work out the oral morphine equivalent, then use this table (label Table 2). The label's Table 1 for named drugs runs higher than converting through the Fast Fact #36 table; the tool uses the lower result, as the label prefers to underestimate.

Stop all other around-the-clock opioids when the patch goes on. Levels are therapeutic only after 18–36 hours, so keep a breakthrough opioid. Do not increase before 3 days; judge further increases after two 3-day patches.

From an IV fentanyl infusion: 1:1 (mcg/h to mcg/h); keep the infusion going 6 hours after the patch goes on, halve it for 6 hours, then stop (one study of 15 inpatients).

Mild to moderate liver or kidney impairment: start at half the dose; severe: avoid. Heat (heating pads, hot baths, saunas) and fever raise absorption; at 40 °C levels could rise by about a third.

Never use these tables backwards. After removal, levels fall by about half in 20–27 hours and the skin keeps releasing drug for at least 24 hours.

Methadone

Switching to methadone

Oral morphine mg/dayMethadone a day, % of that

Why: Methadone gets relatively stronger as the prior dose rises, and it accumulates for days, so the ratio widens and the start is capped.

More

Label: this is not a table of equal doses, and it cannot be used to convert from methadone to another opioid. Deaths have occurred in opioid-tolerant patients during conversion. Stop other around-the-clock opioids; divide the daily dose (for example every 8 hours) and round down. Underestimating is safer.

APS/CPDD guideline: when switching, start at 75–90% less than the calculated equal dose and no higher than 30–40 mg a day; raise by no more than 10 mg a day every 5–7 days. The label allows changes every 3–5 days; the guideline is the stricter.

ECG before starting if there are QTc risk factors (strong), consider in everyone (weak); repeat 2–4 weeks after starting, at 30–40 mg a day and at 100 mg a day. QTc over 500 ms: do not start; 450–500 ms: consider another opioid and correct causes. Torsades is more common above 200 mg a day.

Peak respiratory depression comes later and lasts longer than pain relief; half-life 8–59 hours. Many drugs change methadone levels (CYP3A4, 2B6, 2C19, 2C9, 2D6): check every new drug with a pharmacist. Other tables differ (Fast Fact #75 uses 3:1 under 100 mg morphine up to 20:1 over 1000, and then cuts a further 25–50%).

Opioid-tolerant

Who counts as opioid-tolerant

For a week or longer, at least:

  • oral morphine 60 mg a day
  • fentanyl patch 25 mcg/h
  • oral oxycodone 30 mg a day
  • oral hydromorphone 8 mg a day
  • oral oxymorphone 25 mg a day
  • oral hydrocodone 60 mg a day
  • or an equal dose of another opioid

Why: The patch and long-acting high-strength products assume tolerance; in anyone else the first dose can stop breathing.

More

The tool adds up each opioid as a share of its threshold. IV doses count through the equal-dose table (IV morphine 24 mg, IV hydromorphone 4.8 mg a day each make one full share), which is stricter than the label's own patch table. Tramadol and tapentadol are not counted.

Duragesic label

Risk · CDC 2022

MME and naloxone

  • Offer naloxone when prescribing opioids, particularly with: a history of overdose, a substance use disorder, sleep-disordered breathing, 50 MME a day or more, a benzodiazepine, or a return to a dose they have lost tolerance to (after a taper or release from prison).
  • Before raising the total to 50 MME a day or more, pause and reassess benefit and risk. No dose is free of risk.
  • Use particular caution with opioids and benzodiazepines together.

Why: Overdose risk rises with dose and with anything else that slows breathing.

More

The CDC guideline is for outpatients, including prescriptions at hospital discharge. It does not cover cancer pain, sickle cell disease, palliative or end-of-life care, or inpatient care. Naloxone can also be offered when a household member is at risk.

Tapering: about 10% a month or slower after a year or more of use; never stop abruptly.

Kidneys

Opioids when eGFR is under 30

AvoidCodeine; meperidine (seizures from normeperidine)
CautionMorphine (metabolites build up; minimize long-acting forms), hydromorphone (may be preferable, no clear consensus), oxycodone (accumulates: adjust dose or interval)
Relatively safeFentanyl (no active metabolites), methadone

Why: Morphine and codeine are cleared as active metabolites by the kidney, so they build up and cause sedation and myoclonus.

More

Fast Fact #161 cites dose cuts of 25% for creatinine clearance 10–50 and 50% under 10 for morphine, oxycodone and hydromorphone. The patch label is stricter than the Fast Fact: half dose in mild to moderate kidney impairment, avoid in severe.

When eGFR is under 30 the tool uses the 50% cut for a change of drug (expert practice).

FF161 2024 (expert practice)

After a switch · tap each finding you have

Too sleepy, or in more pain?

Why: After a switch, the timing, the kidneys, heat on a patch and other sedatives separate too much opioid from too little.

More

Check first: breathing rate, oxygen saturation, pupils, sedation level, and every dose given in the last 24 hours including as-needed doses. Then: eGFR, temperature and anything warming a patch, and the drug chart for benzodiazepines and new interacting drugs.

Faded means less likely, never excluded. Treat slow breathing first: naloxone and support breathing.
