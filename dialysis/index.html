Intermittent or prolonged intermittent dose

Deliver a Kt/V of 3.9 per week (KDIGO 2012 5.8.3, 1A), for example 1.3 three times a week (KDIGO rationale). Prescribe the dose before each session and check what was delivered (5.8.1, 1B).

Prolonged intermittent therapy (SLED, PIRRT) usually runs 6 to 10 hours a day (Jang 2020).

What CRRT takes out besides toxins

  • Phosphate. Low phosphate is common (54% at 25 and 65% at 40 mL/kg/h in RENAL). Phosphate-containing CRRT solutions, used by protocol, prevent most of it (Broman 2011; Pistolesi 2019); with standard fluids, check and replace phosphate. The electrolyte tool's "on dialysis" rows are for maintenance intermittent dialysis, not CRRT.
  • Magnesium. Check daily on citrate: citrate binds it as well as calcium.
  • Potassium. Keeps falling while the circuit runs; recheck on the unit's schedule.
  • Heat. Hypothermia is common: 53% reached 35 °C or below in the first 48 hours in one single-centre series, commoner in shock and with hemodiafiltration even though that group had a warmed return line. Check the temperature, and remember the circuit can hide a fever.
  • Drugs. Anything small and not protein-bound comes out. See the Drugs tab.

Recheck schedule and bag choice: adjust to your institution's CRRT protocol.

When to stop

Stop when the kidneys meet the patient's needs, or when dialysis no longer fits the goals of care (KDIGO 2012 5.2.1, not graded).

  • A trial off is reasonable when urine output is over 450 mL a day without diuretics, or over 2300 mL a day with them, and a 2-hour timed creatinine clearance is 23 mL/min or more (KDIGO 2026 draft PP 5.9.3).
  • Urine output predicted success best without diuretics (area under the curve 0.845, against 0.671 with diuretics; BEST Kidney, Uchino 2009). It predicts success; it is not a requirement.
  • Diuretics do not speed recovery or shorten dialysis (KDIGO 2012 5.2.2, 2B); they can still manage fluid around stopping (draft PP 5.9.4).

What decides how much CRRT removes

  • Protein binding. Only the free drug crosses. The sieving coefficient is roughly 1 minus the bound fraction: 0 means not removed, 1 means freely removed.
  • Volume of distribution. Removal matters most for drugs with a small volume, low binding and mainly renal clearance; little for lipophilic drugs cleared mainly outside the kidney (for example linezolid; levofloxacin and ciprofloxacin are exceptions because they are cleared by the kidney).
  • Effluent rate. Blood flow is much higher than effluent flow, so the effluent rate sets drug clearance: more effluent, more removal.
  • Pre-filter replacement dilutes the blood entering the filter and lowers clearance.
  • Low albumin raises the free fraction of highly bound drugs, so more is removed.

Rules that prevent underdosing

  • Loading dose: at least the standard dose. The volume of distribution is usually unchanged or larger in critical illness; CRRT changes only the doses after (Wong 2015).
  • Maintenance: based on total clearance, which moves with the effluent rate. Tell pharmacy the effluent rate and when it changes.
  • Interruptions: a clotted filter, a trip to CT or a bag change lowers delivered clearance; tell pharmacy when CRRT has been off.
  • Levels: draw from the patient or a pre-filter port, never after the filter.
  • Choosing the antibiotic: empiric antibiotics. This tool gives no drug dose table of its own.

1 · How blood is cleaned

Diffusion and convection

Diffusion moves small solutes down a concentration gradient into dialysate. Convection drags solutes along with water pushed across the membrane, which carries larger molecules too.

2 · Why continuous is gentler

The same litres, spread out

The kidney is replaced either fast for a few hours or slowly all day. Blood pressure tolerates the slow version better.

3 · Citrate anticoagulation

Clotting stopped in the circuit only

Citrate takes calcium out of the blood in the circuit; calcium is put back before the blood returns, and the body burns the citrate.

4 · Filtration fraction

Why filters clot

The more plasma water is pulled out of the blood in the filter, the thicker the blood that is left.

5 · Drugs on CRRT

Only free drug crosses

A drug stuck to albumin or hidden in tissue cannot reach the filter.
