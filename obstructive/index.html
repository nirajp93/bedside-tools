On the ventilator

Start with the obstructive settings in the ventilator tool (choose "Asthma or COPD"): tidal volume 6 to 8 mL/kg predicted weight, rate about 12, inspiratory flow 60 to 90 L/min, I:E about 1:4 (1:4 to 1:6 in asthma), minute ventilation about 6 to 8 L/min. Narrative reviews, expert practice

  • Accept a high CO₂: aim for a pH around 7.25 to 7.30; one review accepts down to 7.15. Darioli 1984 (a case series of 34 episodes) ventilated for oxygen rather than CO₂ with no deaths. Expert practice
  • Plateau over 28: cut the minute ventilation. As in the ventilator tool; expert practice
  • Set PEEP: in asthma no more than 5, and disputed; in COPD titrate against the plateau. Expert practice
  • Sudden low pressure: disconnect for 30 to 60 seconds and let the chest empty; if no better, look for tension pneumothorax. Reviews (Gayen 2024)
  • Sedation: deep enough to stop the patient fighting a slow rate (sedation tool). Paralysis only when sedation is not enough, for as short a time as possible; with high-dose steroids it adds a risk of myopathy. Expert practice

What to watch

  • Potassium and magnesium every 4 to 6 hours on high-dose or continuous albuterol, more so with steroids and diuretics; ECG monitoring. Expert practice
  • Lactate can rise from albuterol itself (with warm skin and no shock). Do not read it as sepsis, and do not answer it with more albuterol; it adds acid and drives breathing harder. Expert practice
  • Glucose on steroids. Expert practice
  • Peak flow or the gas within 1 hour of starting treatment, then by response. Expert practice

Two ways the small airways narrow

Asthma: muscle spasm, swelling and mucus, largely reversible with bronchodilators and steroids. COPD: fixed narrowing and loss of the air sacs that hold small airways open, so they collapse when breathing out. Both trap air.
