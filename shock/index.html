What to recheck in 30 to 60 minutes

  • Capillary refill about every 30 minutes while resuscitating, hourly once it is normal (the ANDROMEDA-SHOCK protocol). Press a glass slide on the fingertip for 10 seconds; over 3 seconds is abnormal. Trial protocol
  • Lactate again within about 2 hours if it was raised; follow the trend, not one value. ESICM 2014 serial lactate, level 1C
  • Mean pressure, mentation, hourly urine. Expert practice
  • Before more fluid, test fluid responsiveness in shock that persists after the first fluid (fluids tool). ESICM 2025, good practice statement
  • Repeat the focused echo after each big change (fluid, pressor, intubation), because the type can change. ESICM 2014: echo for sequential evaluation

Pressure target

Start at a mean pressure of 65 mmHg in septic shock and adjust to perfusion; the agent and dose are in the pressors tool. SSC 2026

Bleeding trauma is different: a lower pressure is accepted until bleeding is controlled, and brain injury needs a higher one. Follow the trauma team. Expert practice; trauma guidelines hold the numbers

Definitions

Shock is acute circulatory failure in which cells cannot use enough oxygen. Low blood pressure is usual but not required. ESICM 2014, level 1B

Hypotension: systolic under 90, mean under 65, or a fall of 40 mmHg or more from baseline; it is not required to define shock. ESICM 2014 (not required: level 1B)

Hypoperfusion windows: skin (cold, mottled), kidney (urine under 0.5 mL/kg/h), brain (confusion); lactate is typically over 2 mmol/L. ESICM 2014 Capillary refill is the skin measure to monitor; over 3 s is abnormal. ESICM 2025 good practice; ANDROMEDA-SHOCK protocol

Septic shock (Sepsis-3): a vasopressor is needed to keep the mean pressure 65 or more, and lactate is over 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid. See the sepsis tool. Sepsis-3, 2016

5 · Workup that narrows

What to send, and what each result removes

Send first: lactate, blood gas, blood count, chemistry, troponin, ECG, glucose, type and screen, blood cultures if infection is possible, cortisol (and ACTH) if adrenal crisis is possible, drawn before hydrocortisone but never delaying it, and a focused ultrasound. Only after first results: natriuretic peptide, CT pulmonary angiogram once safe to travel, formal echo, ScvO₂ and CO₂ gap once a central line is in.
