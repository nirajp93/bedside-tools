Oxygen and pH

Crashing on the ventilator

Take the patient off the ventilator and bag by hand

A sudden pressure change with a falling blood pressure or saturation: disconnect, give 100% oxygen, and ventilate through the tube at 8 to 10 breaths a minute. Then work through DOPE.

On high PEEP, disconnecting drops the PEEP: use a bag with a PEEP valve if you have one (expert practice).

Why: Bagging by hand takes the machine out of the question and lets trapped gas escape; how the bag feels tells you whether the problem is the patient or the equipment.

More

Easy to bag and the patient improves: the ventilator or circuit was the problem. Hard to bag: the tube or the patient. Pass a suction catheter, listen to both sides, and look at the tube depth.

High or low pressure alarm

Read peak against plateau

Do an inspiratory hold, then tap what you see. Causes that no longer fit fade.

Why: Peak pressure includes the push needed to move gas down the tube; the plateau, measured with flow stopped, is what the lung itself is under. The gap between them is resistance.

More

The hold is only valid when the patient is making little or no effort. Normal compliance on a ventilator is 60 to 80 mL/cm H2O, and normal airway resistance under 15 cm H2O/L/s with a normal-sized tube.

Air trapping

Trapped gas (auto-PEEP)

    Blood pressure falling with trapped gas: disconnect the ventilator and let the chest empty.

    Why: Trapped gas raises the pressure in the chest, which holds back blood returning to the heart, so the blood pressure falls.

    More

    For missed triggers caused by trapped gas, in COPD with flow limitation, added PEEP up to about 80% of the trapped pressure does not add to the hyperinflation and can help the patient trigger.

    More

    Dyssynchrony in more than one breath in ten has been linked to longer ventilation and more tracheostomy. Look for pain, a blocked tube, trapped gas and wrong settings before adding sedation.

    1 · Size the breath

    Lungs follow height, not weight

    Two men of 175 cm have the same size lungs whether they weigh 60 kg or 120 kg. Tap a weight to see what 6 mL/kg would give if you used it.

    2 · Pressures

    One breath, three numbers

    3 · Driving pressure

    The number that tracked with survival

    Plateau minus PEEP is the pressure swing of each breath. In a pooled analysis of nine trials, each 7 cm H2O more went with 41% higher mortality, and 36% higher when the tidal volume and plateau were already in the safe range.

    One small trial of limiting driving pressure (STAMINA, 2025) found no benefit, so 15 is a working mark, not a rule.

    4 · Permissive hypercapnia

    A high CO2 is the price of a small breath

    In ARDS the protocol answers a falling pH with rate first. Tap a pH.

    This does not apply in brain injury or after cardiac arrest, where the CO2 is kept normal.
