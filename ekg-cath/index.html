Occlusion patterns, mimics, 2025 ACC/AHA timing tiers, and a call script. Wards and ICU.

Method

The 60-second occlusion read

Fixed order, every tracing, before the computer interpretation is consulted. The question is occlusion, not whether a millimetre threshold is met. Tap a label to see what to look for.

1
Context

Symptoms present or resolved; onset time; prior ECG on screen. Rate, rhythm, QRS width. LBBB or ventricular paced → Sgarbossa rules apply.

2
ST in every lead

Measure at the J point against the QRS onset (TP segment if the baseline is stable). Elevation: which contiguous group. Depression maximal V1–V3 → posterior OMI until excluded.

3
T wave and QRS

T wave bulky relative to its own QRS → hyperacute. Biphasic or deep symmetric inversion V2–V3, pain-free → Wellens. Loss of R, terminal QRS distortion → LAD occlusion over early repolarisation.

4
Reciprocity

STE with reciprocal STD is occlusion until proven otherwise. aVL depression is a sensitive early inferior clue. No reciprocal change → consider mimic.

5
Decide, then repeat

Pattern + symptoms → call. Uncertain → V7–V9, V4R, repeat ECG in 15 min. Troponin confirms; it does not gate the call.

Why reciprocal depression is the discriminator

Transmural injury produces a current that points toward the infarcting wall. Leads viewing from the opposite side record the same vector pointing away from them: depression. Pericarditis and early repolarisation inflame or repolarise diffusely and have no single injury vector, so they rarely produce reciprocal depression outside aVR and V1.

Corollary: an inferior STE pattern with depression in aVL is not pericarditis.

Why V2–V3 are different

Young men carry 1–2 mm of concave anterior STE as a normal variant. The threshold rises so the rule does not fire on physiology; women have less baseline STE, so the bar is lower. The criteria assume no LBBB, paced rhythm, or LVH; in those settings use the Smith-modified Sgarbossa criteria (Call tab).

    Script

    The 45-second call

    Have in hand · do not

    Have in hand: ECG timed and filed, prior ECG pulled; two large-bore IVs; pads on if unstable; aspirin 162–325 mg chewed; weight, creatinine, Hgb, platelets, INR if on warfarin; anticoagulant and last dose; PDE5-inhibitor use (no nitrates within 24–48 h); contrast allergy; code status and goals.

    Do not: wait for troponin to call a STEMI; give nitrates to an RV infarct or a hypotensive patient; give fibrinolytics for NSTE-ACS (Class 3: harm) except true posterior MI; load a P2Y12 inhibitor before the lab confirms unless the local pathway says so.

    Smith-modified Sgarbossa (LBBB or paced)

    Why a ratio, not 5 mm

    In LBBB the ST segment is normally discordant, opposite the dominant QRS deflection, and its magnitude scales with the QRS. A fixed 5 mm rule fires on big QRS complexes and misses small ones. Discordant STE ≥ 25% of the preceding S-wave depth marks excess beyond expected discordance. Concordance of any size is abnormal. Any single criterion is positive (Smith 2012; sensitivity ≈ 80%, specificity ≈ 99% in the validation cohort). A new LBBB alone is no longer a STEMI equivalent.
