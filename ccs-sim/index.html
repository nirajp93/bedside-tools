CCS case simulator

Step 3 case practice. Type orders, move the clock, read results as they come back, place the patient, then see what you did, when, and why it matters.

How a case runs

  • Read the opening screen. Nothing happens until you order it: monitoring and pulse oximetry included.
  • Type orders (three letters bring up matches). Medicines are ordered by name; this simulator, like the exam, does not ask for doses.
  • Move the clock to get results. Tests take simulated time; examining the patient takes a few minutes.
  • Move the patient to the right place, then end the case and read the review.

    3 · What scores

    The right thing, soon enough, in order

    • Credit goes to indicated actions, judged by timing and sequence. A correct step made late or out of order may earn nothing.
    • Actions that are not indicated, or carry risk, lower the score. Cost counts indirectly, through how inappropriate an order is.
    • Being aggressive when watching and waiting is the standard of care also scores lower.
    • Counselling, diet and activity orders tend to carry little or no weight.

    From the USMLE description of CCS scoring. The NBME does not publish its keys; this simulator's grading is its own.

    4 · Opening orders

    Stabilise, then the test that changes the plan

    Expert practice: a way to order, not a rule the exam publishes.

    5 · Ending a case

    Finish the plan before you leave

    • When the case ends you get a few minutes to add or cancel orders. After that you cannot examine, move the patient, book follow-up or see pending results.
    • Use those minutes for what a discharge or handover needs: the long-term medicine, the counselling that matters for this patient, the follow-up.
    • On the exam you must also select Exit Case. Here, End case opens the review.

    For education only. Not a substitute for clinical judgment. Cases are invented for practice, simplified, and written for adults in US practice.
