8 · Find the cause

What to send, and how each result narrows it

Send first:

  • Lipase (or amylase), liver tests with bilirubin, triglycerides, calcium
  • Abdominal ultrasound for gallstones, in every patient
  • An alcohol history: amount and years

Only after a result or a course:

  • MRCP or EUS if a duct stone is suspected without cholangitis
  • CT or MRI if not improving at 48 to 72 hours
  • No cause: repeat ultrasound and fasting triglycerides as an outpatient; MRI or EUS; over 40, look for a tumor

The trials behind the practice

Most are Dutch multicentre trials; numbers below are from the published abstracts. WATERFALL stopped early, so its estimates are wide.

For education only. Not a substitute for clinical judgment. Adults only; not for chronic pancreatitis, pregnancy or children. The ACG 2024 guideline is the main source; the IAP/APA 2025 revision is shown where ACG is silent (pain, triglyceride treatment, intensive care steps). Evidence tiers are each guideline's own: ACG, AGA and IAP strength (strong or conditional) and quality (high to very low); ACG key concepts and AGA 2020 best practice advice are ungraded; WSACS GRADE 1 or 2 with A to D; rows marked expert practice are not from a graded statement. WATERFALL fluid numbers are the trial's protocol and apply to the patients it enrolled. Insulin and heparin protocols for high triglycerides, analgesic choice and antibiotic choice vary: adjust to your institution.
