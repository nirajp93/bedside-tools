Where is the fluid?

Five questions a focused exam can answer, and what it cannot.

1How well does the LV squeeze?

Say it in broad words: hyperdynamic, normal, reduced or severely reduced. Judge it in at least two views. No ejection-fraction number.

Why: broad categories are what a focused exam supports. A number implies a precision it does not have.

More

What to watch: the walls thicken, the inner edge moves inward, and the front mitral leaflet opens toward the septum. Expert practice.

E-point septal separation (M-mode at the mitral tips in the long-axis view): over 7 mm was 100% sensitive (95% CI 63 to 100) and 52% specific (39 to 65) for an EF of 30% or less. Read it one way only: 7 mm or less makes a severely reduced EF unlikely. Single centre, convenience sample (McKaigney 2014).

An apical view cut short of the true apex makes the LV look better than it is.

ASE 2013 focused cardiac ultrasound statement; international FoCUS recommendations 2014 (consensus).

2Is the RV big, and is the septum flat?

The RV should look no more than two-thirds the size of the LV, and the LV should form the apex. A septum that flattens in systole means pressure load; in diastole, volume load; in both, pressure with or without volume.

Why: the septum bows toward whichever ventricle has the lower pressure at that moment.

A normal-looking RV does not exclude pulmonary embolism. Pooled sensitivity of right heart strain on echo was 53%, specificity 83%.
More

TAPSE (M-mode through the lateral tricuspid annulus, RV-focused apical view): over 1.7 cm normal; 1.3 to 1.7 mild; 1.0 to 1.3 moderate; 1.0 or less severe. Never read it alone: it misleads after cardiac surgery, with pacing leads or catheters, in constriction, with severe tricuspid regurgitation, or with a misaligned cursor.

RV basal diameter under 4.1 cm is normal (end-diastole, RV-focused view). RV free wall under 5 mm is normal; a thick wall points to long-standing load but is not specific. RV size needs more than one view.

ASE right heart guideline 2025. Embolism figures: Fields 2017, meta-analysis.

3Is there fluid around the heart?

A dark band around the heart, measured at end-diastole: 10 to 20 mm is moderate, over 20 mm is large. In the long-axis view pericardial fluid lies in front of the descending aorta; pleural fluid lies behind it.

Why: size does not define tamponade. Pressure does, and a small fluid collection that arrives fast can be enough.

Tamponade is neither a bedside nor an ultrasound diagnosis alone. An effusion on the screen is not tamponade until the patient fits.
More

Signs that support tamponade: the right atrium collapses in systole (earliest); the right ventricle collapses in diastole (high specificity); the IVC is full with little change on breathing (high sensitivity); mitral and tricuspid inflow swing with breathing more than normal.

Size: ESC 2025 myocarditis and pericarditis guideline. Signs: Alerhand 2019, narrative review. Aorta landmark: expert practice.

4What does the IVC say about right atrial pressure?

3mm Hg8mm Hg15mm Hg20consider

More

Measure in the subcostal long-axis view, 0.5 to 3 cm from the right atrium. Use a sniff; quiet breathing if the patient cannot sniff. M-mode times the change better.

A large IVC can be normal in young adults, athletes and pregnancy.

ASE right heart guideline 2025.

5Is there an obvious valve problem?

Flag only: a leaflet that is thick, fixed or flailing, or a large colour jet. Do not grade it. Any flag means a formal echo.

Why: a focused exam finds the obvious and misses the rest.

More

Ten hospitalists with hand-carried ultrasound, 354 inpatients: LV function matched the cardiologist's read exactly in 59% with ultrasound against 46% by exam, and within one level in 88% against 67%. There was no gain for aortic stenosis, aortic regurgitation or mitral regurgitation.

ASE 2013 (grading valve lesions is not appropriate for a focused exam); Martin 2009, single centre.

Limits

What this exam does not settle

  • A new abnormal finding, or a normal scan in a patient who is still unexplained, gets a formal echo.
  • Do not call these at the bedside: aortic dissection, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, regional wall motion abnormality, LV aneurysm or thrombus, masses, vegetations. A scan that looks normal does not exclude them.
  • Pulmonary pressures, diastolic function and constriction are outside a focused exam.

ASE 2013; international FoCUS recommendations 2014.
