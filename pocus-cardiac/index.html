Where is the fluid?

Five questions a focused exam can answer, and what it cannot.

1How well does the LV squeeze?

Say it in broad words: hyperdynamic, normal, reduced or severely reduced. Judge it in more than one view when you can. A number is not needed.

Why: broad categories are what a focused exam supports. A number implies a precision it does not have.

More

What to watch: the walls thicken, the inner edge moves inward, and the front mitral leaflet opens toward the septum. Expert practice.

E-point septal separation (M-mode at the mitral tips in the long-axis view): over 7 mm was 100% sensitive (95% CI 63 to 100) and 52% specific (39 to 65) for an EF of 30% or less. Read it one way only: 7 mm or less makes a severely reduced EF unlikely. Single centre, convenience sample (McKaigney 2014).

An apical view cut short of the true apex makes the LV look better than it is.

ASE 2013 focused cardiac ultrasound statement; international FoCUS recommendations 2014 (consensus).

2Is the RV big, and is the septum flat?

The RV should look no more than two-thirds the size of the LV, and the LV should form the apex. A septum that flattens in systole means pressure load; in diastole, volume load; in both, pressure with or without volume.

Why: the septum bows toward whichever ventricle has the lower pressure at that moment.

A normal-looking RV does not exclude pulmonary embolism. Pooled sensitivity of right heart strain on echo was 53%, specificity 83%.
More

TAPSE (M-mode through the lateral tricuspid annulus, RV-focused apical view): over 1.7 cm normal; over 1.3 to 1.7 mild; over 1.0 to 1.3 moderate; 1.0 or less severe. Never read it alone: it misleads after cardiac surgery, with pacing leads or catheters, in constriction, with severe tricuspid regurgitation, or with a misaligned cursor.

RV basal diameter under 4.1 cm is normal (end-diastole, RV-focused view). RV free wall under 5 mm is normal. RV size needs more than one view.

ASE right heart guideline 2025. Embolism figures: Fields 2017, meta-analysis.

3Is there fluid around the heart?

A dark band around the heart, measured at end-diastole: 10 to 20 mm is moderate, over 20 mm is large. In the long-axis view pericardial fluid lies in front of the descending aorta; pleural fluid lies behind it.

Why: size does not define tamponade. Pressure does, and a small fluid collection that arrives fast can be enough.

Tamponade is neither a bedside nor an ultrasound diagnosis alone. An effusion on the screen is not tamponade until the patient fits.
More

Signs that support tamponade: the right atrium collapses in systole (earliest); the right ventricle collapses in diastole (high specificity); the IVC is full with little change on breathing (high sensitivity); mitral and tricuspid inflow swing with breathing more than normal.

Size: ESC 2025 myocarditis and pericarditis guideline. Signs: Alerhand 2019, narrative review. Aorta landmark: expert practice.

4What does the IVC say about right atrial pressure?

3mm Hg8mm Hg15mm Hg20consider

More

Measure in the subcostal long-axis view, 0.5 to 3 cm from the right atrium. Use a sniff; quiet breathing if the patient cannot sniff. M-mode times the change better.

A large IVC can be normal in young adults, athletes and pregnancy.

ASE right heart guideline 2025.

5Is there an obvious valve problem?

Flag only: a leaflet that is thick, fixed or flailing, or a large colour jet. Do not grade it. Any flag means a formal echo.

Why: a focused exam finds the obvious and misses the rest.

More

Ten hospitalists with hand-carried ultrasound, 354 inpatients: LV function matched the cardiologist's read exactly in 59% with ultrasound against 46% by exam, and within one level in 88% against 67%. There was no gain for aortic stenosis, aortic regurgitation or mitral regurgitation.

ASE 2013 (grading valve lesions is not appropriate for a focused exam); Martin 2009, single centre.

Limits

What this exam does not settle

  • A new abnormal finding, or a normal scan in a patient who is still unexplained, gets a formal echo.
  • Do not call these at the bedside: aortic dissection, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, regional wall motion abnormality, LV aneurysm or thrombus, masses, vegetations. A scan that looks normal does not exclude them.
  • Pulmonary pressures, diastolic function and constriction are outside a focused exam.
More

Not every mobile echo on a valve is a vegetation. Redundant chordae are thin and string-like, as bright as pericardium, move with the valve, and usually come without new regurgitation. This is why a focused exam neither calls nor excludes endocarditis.

Expert practice; no primary source.

ASE 2013; international FoCUS recommendations 2014.

    Windows

    Where the probe goes

    More

    Parasternal and subcostal windows are preferred for a focused exam. Apical views are harder to get, and a view cut short of the true apex overestimates LV function. ASE 2013.

    RV-focused apical view: with the probe at the apex, rotate until the RV looks its largest. Used for RV basal diameter and TAPSE. ASE right heart guideline 2025.

    Apical five-chamber: tilt the probe forward from the four-chamber view to open the LV outflow tract. Pulsed Doppler goes about 1 cm below the aortic valve, parallel to flow. Blanco 2020, review.

    Probe positions are expert practice.

    Pitfall

    Is the picture mirrored?

    Before reading an apical view, confirm which side is which. The marker dot on the screen and the marker on the probe must agree with the preset.

    Why: a mirrored apical view puts the LV where the RV is expected, so a failing LV can be mistaken for a dilated RV and treated as pulmonary embolism.

    More

    Check the anatomy, not the side of the screen: the right ventricle has the moderator band and the valve that sits closer to the apex; the descending aorta lies beside the left atrium.

    Expert practice.

    Fellow level

    E-point septal separation

    M-mode at the mitral tips in the long-axis view. Measure the smallest gap between the open front leaflet and the septum. 7 mm or less makes a severely reduced EF unlikely. Over 7 mm needs confirmation.

    Why: over 7 mm was 100% sensitive but only 52% specific for an EF of 30% or less, so it rules out better than it rules in.

    More

    It misleads when the leaflet cannot open fully or the septum is thick: mitral stenosis, aortic regurgitation, septal hypertrophy. Expert practice.

    McKaigney 2014: single centre, convenience sample, three operators.

    Evidence

    What the studies showed

    SHoC-ED, 2018

    Question: does an ultrasound protocol improve survival in undifferentiated hypotension? Design: randomised, 6 centres, 273 patients. Result: survival to 30 days or discharge 104 of 136 with ultrasound against 102 of 134 without; difference 0.35% (95% CI -10.2 to 11.0). No difference in CT use, fluids, inotropes or length of stay. What it changed: tempers the claim that a shock protocol saves lives.

    Atkinson, Ann Emerg Med 2018;72:478-89, doi:10.1016/j.annemergmed.2018.04.002.

    Fields, 2017

    Question: how accurate is echo for pulmonary embolism? Design: meta-analysis. Result: right heart strain: sensitivity 53% (45 to 61), specificity 83% (74 to 90). What it changed: echo can support pulmonary embolism at the bedside and never excludes it.

    J Am Soc Echocardiogr 2017;30:714-23, doi:10.1016/j.echo.2017.03.004.

    Martin, 2009

    Question: does hand-carried ultrasound help hospitalists beyond the exam? Design: 10 hospitalists, 354 inpatients, one centre. Result: LV function matched the cardiologist's read exactly in 59% with ultrasound against 46% by exam (P=.005), within one level in 88% against 67%. No gain for aortic stenosis, aortic regurgitation or mitral regurgitation. What it changed: use it for LV function; do not grade valves.

    Am J Med 2009;122:35-41, doi:10.1016/j.amjmed.2008.07.022.

    McKaigney, 2014

    Question: does E-point septal separation find a severely reduced EF? Design: convenience sample of patients having a formal echo, scanned by three emergency ultrasound fellows. Result: over 7 mm: sensitivity 100% (63 to 100), specificity 52% (39 to 65) for an EF of 30% or less. What it changed: a rule-out, not a rule-in. Weak evidence: the sensitivity rests on very few patients with a low EF.

    Am J Emerg Med 2014;32:493-7, doi:10.1016/j.ajem.2014.01.045.

    ASE right heart guideline, 2025

    What this page takes from it: TAPSE is graded mild, moderate or severe, with over 1.7 cm normal. Right atrial pressure is reported as 3, 8 or 15 mm Hg, and 20 can be considered.

    Mukherjee, J Am Soc Echocardiogr 2025;38:141-86, doi:10.1016/j.echo.2025.01.006.
