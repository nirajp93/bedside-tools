1 · Oxygen

Hemoglobin is a carrier, not a target

Drag the hemoglobin. The oxygen each decilitre of blood carries falls in a straight line, but the body has slack: at rest it uses about a quarter of what is delivered.

2 · The trials

Restrictive was as safe, with less blood

Each trial compared a lower trigger (filled) with a higher one (ring). Tap a trial.

3 · The exception

The ischemic heart is different

Most tissues take more oxygen out of the blood when hemoglobin falls. The heart muscle already takes out most of it at rest, so it can only get more by more coronary flow. Tap each state.

4 · Platelets

Four numbers: 10, 20, 50, or none

The threshold rises with the bleeding risk of what is about to happen. Tap a number.

6 · Narrowing

Platelets given, count did not rise

Send a count 10 to 60 minutes after the next dose and another at about 24 hours, and work out the corrected count increment (CCI; calculator on the Platelets, plasma tab). Tap each result as it comes back.

7 · Evidence

The trials behind the numbers

For education only. Not a substitute for clinical judgment. For adults. Pregnancy is covered only where marked (obstetric bleeding, neuraxial counts). Out of scope: children, antenatal anemia, transfusion-dependent anemia (thalassemia, sickle cell disease), exchange transfusion, transfusion reactions and blood-bank product selection (irradiated, washed, HLA-matched). Product doses and expected rises are averages from the US Circular of Information; your blood bank's products and protocol come first.
