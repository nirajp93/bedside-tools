Cause

Avoid at every tier

  • Hypoxia and low blood pressure.
  • Hypotonic fluid and a falling sodium.
  • Fever.
  • Anything tight around the neck, or the head turned to one side.
  • Prolonged or preventive hyperventilation: a PaCO2 of 25 or less for long periods is not recommended (BTF, level IIB), and not below 30 routinely (SIBICC).
  • Osmotherapy on a clock. Give a bolus for a rise in pressure or a new sign.
  • Steroids for swelling from trauma, bleeding or stroke. They treat tumor edema; in meningitis they reduce sequelae, not ICP (Causes tab).

Suspected meningitis: give antibiotics and dexamethasone first; do not wait for the CT. When a CT is needed before lumbar puncture, do the puncture only if it shows no mass effect.

Expert practice. A normal CT does not exclude raised pressure.

Steroids: where they help and where they harm

Help: tumor edema, and bacterial or TB meningitis. Harm or no benefit: trauma, bleeding, stroke swelling.

Why: steroids shrink vasogenic edema, the leaky-barrier swelling around a tumor, but not the cell swelling of injury or infarction; in head injury they raised deaths.

More

Tumor: high-dose corticosteroids for vasogenic edema from a brain tumor (ENLS). Dose by neurosurgery or oncology. If lymphoma is possible, ask before giving them: steroids can make the biopsy non-diagnostic. Impending herniation comes first.

Bacterial meningitis: dexamethasone 10 mg IV every 6 hours for 4 days, before or with the first antibiotic, to reduce hearing loss and other sequelae; 0.15 mg/kg every 6 hours for low body weight. TB meningitis: steroids reduce deaths (strong), continued for two weeks or more (conditional). Neither is an ICP treatment.

Trauma: not recommended; high-dose methylprednisolone raised mortality (CRASH) and is contraindicated (BTF level I). Intracerebral hemorrhage: not for raised pressure (AHA/ASA 2022, 3: no benefit; NCS 2020, strong against). Stroke swelling: should not be given (AHA/ASA 2026, 3: harm).

The skull is a fixed box

Brain, blood and CSF share a space that cannot grow. As something new is added, CSF and venous blood move out and the pressure barely changes, until that reserve runs out. Drag to add volume.

Perfusion pressure is what the brain feels

Blood reaches the brain at the arterial pressure minus the pressure in the skull. A healthy brain keeps its flow steady across a range of pressures; an injured one often cannot. Tap a state.

CO2 sets the width of the vessels

Breathing off CO2 narrows the brain's arteries. Less blood in the skull lowers the pressure within a minute, but less blood also reaches the brain. That is why it is a bridge, not a treatment. Tap a PaCO2.

Pulling water out of the brain

Mannitol and hypertonic saline stay in the blood where the blood-brain barrier is intact, so water follows them out of brain tissue. Where the barrier is broken the gradient is weaker. Tap an agent.

Where the brain goes when it moves

The falx and the tentorium divide the skull. Pressure pushes brain past their edges, and each route squeezes something different, so each has its own sign. Tap a route.

Why a ladder

Each tier adds treatments with more risk. You climb when the tier below has failed, and you check the basics and ask about surgery each time you step up. Tap a tier to see what it adds.
