Beyond the heart

Lungs, liver, kidneys, blood, glands, brain

Lungs. Risk rises with age over 60, COPD, heart failure, functional dependence, sleep apnea, low albumin, and upper abdominal, thoracic or long operations. Lower it with smoking cessation, lung expansion, CPAP and early mobilization. No routine chest film or spirometry.

Liver. Child-Pugh and MELD estimate mortality. Child C: avoid elective surgery. Acute hepatitis: delay.

Kidney. CKD raises the risk of AKI. Avoid NSAIDs and hypotension, dose drugs for kidney function, and time dialysis for the day before.

Anemia. Treat iron deficiency, by vein if surgery is weeks away. Transfuse restrictively.

Thyroid and adrenal. Mild hypothyroidism: proceed. Severe hypothyroidism or overt hyperthyroidism: control first. Long-term steroids: see Meds.

Frailty and delirium. Frailty predicts complications better than age. Screen cognition, avoid benzodiazepines and anticholinergics, and discuss goals of care.

Why SGLT2 inhibitors are held

Sugar spills into urine, so blood glucose falls↓Insulin falls and glucagon rises↓Fat breakdown feeds ketone production; the kidney holds on to ketones↓Ketoacidosis with a glucose under 250: it hides

Surgery adds fasting, stress hormones, less insulin and volume loss. An anion gap with a near-normal glucose after surgery: send a serum beta-hydroxybutyrate (urine dipsticks miss it) and treat with insulin plus dextrose from the start.

What counts as high bleeding risk

Major surgery with extensive tissue injury or lasting over 45 minutes; cancer surgery; major orthopedic surgery; cardiac, thoracic, intracranial and spinal surgery; urologic and gastrointestinal surgery, including bowel resection; kidney biopsy; any procedure with neuraxial anesthesia.

Minimal-risk procedures (cataract, minor dental work, minor skin procedures) usually go ahead without stopping the anticoagulant, or with only the dose on the day held.

PAUSE 2019; CHEST 2022

INR mostly tracks factor VII (half-life about 6 hours), but clotting rests on prothrombin (factor II, 60 to 72 hours). That is why the effect takes about 5 days to wear off, and why a normal INR lags behind. Oral vitamin K lets the liver make new factors over 24 to 48 hours; 4-factor PCC supplies them directly, for emergencies.

How long to wait for elective surgery

03 mo6 mo9 mo12 mo

Solid: avoid elective surgery. Outlined: delay if you can; if surgery is time-sensitive, decide with cardiology or neurology.

Why: a new stent is bare metal until the vessel lining grows over it, and stopping antiplatelets in that window invites stent thrombosis just as surgery makes blood clot more easily.

More

Surgery that cannot wait may be considered from 3 months after a drug-eluting stent, if the harm of delay outweighs the cardiac risk (2b). If surgery cannot wait, keep dual antiplatelet therapy going when the bleeding risk allows, especially within 30 days of a bare-metal stent or 3 months of a drug-eluting stent. If the P2Y12 inhibitor must stop, continue aspirin and restart the second drug as soon as possible. A patient on an anticoagulant alone after remote PCI may take aspirin until the anticoagulant resumes.

After a stroke, the excess risk of a second stroke and cardiac events falls over time and levelled off at about 9 months in a Danish registry (Jørgensen, JAMA 2014).

Conditions that change the plan

Severe aortic stenosis

Symptomatic, or meeting valve criteria: evaluate for valve replacement before elective surgery. No symptoms, normal LV function and an echo within a year: low-risk elective surgery may proceed (2a). For larger operations, decide with cardiology and anesthesia and plan close hemodynamic monitoring.

Why: a fixed valve cannot raise output, so pressure depends on preload and filling time. Avoid hypotension and tachycardia.

Heart failure

Decompensated: delay and treat. Compensated: optimize volume, continue guideline therapy, and hold the SGLT2 inhibitor (see Meds).

Why: fluid shifts and the catecholamine surge of surgery tip a heart with little reserve into congestion or low output.

Pacemakers and defibrillators

Know the device, why it was placed, and whether the patient is pacer-dependent. Agree a plan for electrocautery with anesthesia: a magnet or reprogramming, tachycardia therapies off for defibrillators, then back on before the patient leaves monitoring. Interference matters most for surgery above the navel. A magnet on a defibrillator stops shocks but does not change how it paces, so a pacer-dependent patient with a defibrillator needs reprogramming.

Why: cautery noise can be read as the heart's own beat (pacing stops) or as ventricular fibrillation (a shock).

Pulmonary hypertension, congenital heart disease

High risk and not captured by RCRI. Involve the specialist team and a center with experience.

ACC/AHA 2024, reaffirmed 2026

After surgery: troponin and silent injury

Most perioperative heart injury is painless under analgesia. In high-risk patients, a troponin on postoperative days 1 and 2 can be considered to find it.

Why: injury is usually a supply and demand mismatch from hypotension, tachycardia and anemia, and it predicts 30-day mortality even without symptoms (VISION).

More

If troponin rises: look for and treat the cause, get an ECG, and arrange follow-up for risk-factor control (ACC/AHA: reasonable, 2a). ACC/AHA says antithrombotic therapy may be considered (2b); the Canadian guideline recommends starting aspirin and a statin. Write the consult as a risk estimate ("elevated risk, RCRI 1; no testing indicated"), not as "cleared".

ACC/AHA 2024 (2b for surveillance) · CCS 2017 · VISION

Worked case: urgent cervical decompression

A 70-year-old woman with heart failure (EF 45%, non-ischemic), atrial fibrillation, hypertension and type 2 diabetes has progressive cervical myelopathy with grip strength 3/5. Neurosurgery plans decompression and fusion within days.

She rode a recumbent bike daily and climbed the two flights to her apartment without stopping until the weakness began. Weight 72 kg; her last echo was 8 months ago. Medications: metformin, lisinopril, metoprolol succinate, furosemide, warfarin. HR 62, BP 128/82, lungs clear, 1+ pedal edema. Creatinine 0.85, hemoglobin 12.5, A1c 7.6, INR 2.6. ECG: rate-controlled AF. Amoxicillin causes a rash.
