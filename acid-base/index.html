Three defences of pH

1 · What sets pH

pH follows a ratio

HCO3 is the kidney's side, PaCO2 the lungs'. Pick a disorder: the first change, then the other side answering.

2 · Expected answer

Compensation has a size

Each primary change predicts how far the other side should move. A value outside the range means a second disorder.

3 · The gap

What replaced the HCO3?

Positive and negative charges always balance. Pick a state to see which anion took HCO3's place.

4 · Third disorder

Did the gap rise as much as HCO3 fell?

5 · Normal-gap acidosis

Is the kidney excreting acid?

The kidney gets rid of acid as ammonium, which leaves with chloride. The urine anion gap is an indirect read of it.

Causes

By pattern

High-gap acidosis (Berend's GOLD MARRK)

Glycols (ethylene, propylene) · 5-oxoproline (chronic acetaminophen) · L-lactate · D-lactate · methanol · aspirin · renal failure · rhabdomyolysis · ketoacidosis (diabetic, alcoholic, starvation).

Normal-gap acidosis

GI loss (diarrhea, ostomy, pancreatic or biliary drain, ureteral diversion) · proximal RTA · distal RTA · type 4 RTA (low aldosterone) · early kidney disease · saline or chloride load, parenteral nutrition · acetazolamide · recovery from DKA.

Metabolic alkalosis

Urine Cl under 25: vomiting or NG suction, past diuretics, after hypercapnia. Over 40 with high BP: primary aldosteronism, Cushing, licorice, renovascular disease. Over 40 with normal or low BP: current diuretics, Bartter, Gitelman, severe low K or Mg.

Respiratory acidosis (expert practice)

Less drive (opioids, sedatives, brainstem), weak muscles (neuromuscular disease), chest wall (obesity, kyphoscoliosis), airways and lungs (COPD, severe asthma).

Respiratory alkalosis (expert practice)

Low oxygen, pain, anxiety, sepsis, pulmonary embolism, salicylate, liver failure, pregnancy, overventilation on a ventilator.
