Mimics · workup that narrows

When the story does not fit, look for a mimic

Send first: echo and BNP; CT chest; blood count (hemoglobin drop, eosinophils); a full medication history; cultures and respiratory pathogen tests; ANCA, anti-GBM and ANA when no cause is found.

Send after: bronchoscopy with lavage in sequential aliquots, cell count and differential, cultures including Pneumocystis, and cytology, when the CT and first tests do not explain it.

Tap a result. Causes that no longer fit fade.

Why: In one series 7.5% of patients meeting ARDS criteria had no common trigger; many had immune, drug or malignant disease, and the steroid-responsive ones did better.

More

Gibelin 2016 (two French centers, intubated patients, retrospective): of 50 patients with no common risk factor, 36% had immune disease, 26% drug-induced lung injury, 14% malignancy and 24% no cause found. ICU mortality was 66%; lesions that respond to steroids were linked to survival.

Diffuse alveolar hemorrhage: lavage returns get bloodier with each aliquot, or 20% or more of macrophages carry hemosiderin. The second sign is not specific: 7 of 21 patients with biopsy-proven diffuse alveolar damage also met it. Acute eosinophilic pneumonia: more than 25% eosinophils in the lavage, after infection and drugs are excluded.

Heart failure and ARDS can coexist: under the global definition ARDS can still be diagnosed with cardiogenic edema if a trigger is present and the edema is not mainly cardiac.

1 · Two kinds of edema

ARDS is a leak, not a flood

Fluid can reach the air spaces because the pressure pushing it is too high, or because the wall holding it back is broken. Tap each.

2 · The baby lung

The open lung is small

In ARDS the breath goes only to the part that is still open, often a fraction of the lung. Tap a breath size.

Size the breath on Ventilator basics

3 · Prone positioning

Turn the patient, move the collapse

The weight of the heart and the wet lung squeezes the back of the lung when the patient lies on the back. Tap a position.

4 · Shunt and inhaled vasodilators

Send the blood to where the air is

Low oxygen in ARDS is mostly shunt: blood passing alveoli that hold no air. Tap a treatment.

5 · The right ventricle

The thin-walled pump that ARDS loads

Collapse, a high CO2 and high airway pressure all raise the resistance the right ventricle pumps against. Tap a state.
