Why: Nothing that lasts works fast. Saline and calcitonin buy the two to three days an antiresorptive needs.

More

Pamidronate 60–90 mg IV over 2–24 h is the alternative bisphosphonate. Cinacalcet starts at 30 mg twice daily and is used mainly for parathyroid carcinoma, where either a calcimimetic or an antiresorptive is reasonable and surgery is the definitive treatment.

Furosemide lowers calcium only 0.5–1 mg/dL and is not routine; it is for volume overload after fluids, not for calcium.

Primary hyperparathyroidism: who gets surgery

Symptomatic disease, or any one of these without symptoms:

  • Calcium more than 1 mg/dL above the upper limit of normal
  • T-score −2.5 or lower at any site, or a vertebral fracture on imaging
  • eGFR or creatinine clearance below 60 mL/min
  • Kidney stones or nephrocalcinosis on imaging
  • Urine calcium above 250 mg/day (women) or 300 mg/day (men)
  • Age under 50

Why: Each criterion marks an organ already being harmed (bone, kidney) or a long enough life ahead for harm to accumulate.

More

Diagnosis: adjusted calcium high with PTH high or inappropriately normal, on two occasions at least 2 weeks apart.

Before deciding: three-site DXA including the distal one-third radius, vertebral imaging, 24-hour urine calcium, kidney imaging, 25-OH vitamin D, creatinine.

If not operated: calcium and 25-OH vitamin D yearly, kidney function yearly, DXA every 1–2 years. Keep 25-OH vitamin D above 30 ng/mL and below the lab's upper limit. Cinacalcet lowers calcium; alendronate or denosumab protects bone density.

Surgery is not recommended to improve cognition, quality of life or cardiovascular outcomes; the evidence is inconclusive.

Fifth International Workshop 2022

FHH or primary hyperparathyroidism?

Calcium/creatinine clearance ratio from a 24-hour urine.

Ratio: enter all four

Why: In FHH the calcium sensor in the kidney is set too high, so the kidney holds on to calcium even though the blood level is raised.

More

Ratio = (urine Ca × serum creatinine) ÷ (serum Ca × urine creatinine). Below 0.01 favors FHH; above 0.02 favors primary hyperparathyroidism. About 40% of either condition falls between 0.01 and 0.02, and up to 20% of primary hyperparathyroidism falls below 0.01, and up to 10% of FHH falls above 0.02, so the ratio is a reason for genetic testing, not a diagnosis.

Suspect FHH in a young patient or with a family history of high calcium. Thiazides, low vitamin D and kidney impairment lower urine calcium and blur the ratio.

Narrow the differential

Why: PTH splits every calcium problem in two: either the gland is the cause or it is responding. Phosphate and the vitamin D levels then sort what is left.

More

Send with the first draw: repeat calcium with albumin or an ionized calcium, PTH, phosphate, magnesium, creatinine, 25-OH vitamin D.

Send only after the PTH is back: if suppressed, PTHrP, 1,25-(OH)2 vitamin D, SPEP and free light chains, TSH. If high or normal, a 24-hour urine calcium and creatinine.

Faded means unlikely or less likely, never excluded: two causes can coexist, a positive test always outranks a PTH that points the other way, and phosphate is a soft clue that rises with kidney injury. Only a normal ionized calcium crosses causes out. Acute alkalosis lowers ionized calcium alone and is not on the list.
