Is it real?

Pseudohyperkalemia

Potassium that leaked out of cells after the blood was drawn. Serum higher than plasma by more than 0.4 mmol/L defines it (UKKA).

    UKKA: lithium heparin is the sample of choice (1B); paired lithium heparin and clotted samples when pseudohyperkalemia is suspected (1A); a blood-gas potassium in an emergency (1B).

Emergency doses follow the UK Kidney Association guideline (2023; updated July 2026) and match the severe hyperkalemia card in the code cards tool; KDIGO 2020 and AHA 2025 are shown where they differ. Potassium binder doses follow the current US labels. Calcium, insulin and albuterol are used here outside their US labels: the calcium labels are for low calcium and give slower maximum rates, and the albuterol dose is several times the asthma dose. Order sets, binders on formulary and dialysis access vary, so adjust to your institution.
