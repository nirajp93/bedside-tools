The shape of an ICU presentation

One line, overnight, then system by system, then the plan for today

  1. One-liner: age, why they are in the ICU, ICU day, and the main support they are on (ventilator, pressor, dialysis).
  2. Overnight events first, so the team hears what changed before the numbers.
  3. Systems in the same order every day, each ending with an assessment (better, same or worse) and a plan.
  4. Close with the plan for today, code status and who was updated.

Why: a fixed order means nothing is skipped when the patient has ten problems, and the team can find each number where they expect it.

More

Some units present by problem rather than by system. Keep the system sweep as your private check even then: most missed items (feeding, a line on day 9, an antibiotic with no stop date) sit in systems that are not the main problem. Give the trend, not the raw list: "pressor down from 12 to 4 overnight" says more than "norepinephrine 4".

Daily checklist

Before you leave each bed, every item has an answer

Why: the bundle items prevent harm that builds up a day at a time (deep sedation, delirium, weakness, infection, bleeding, clots), and each is easy to forget when the main problem is loud.

More

The list follows the ICU Liberation (A to F) bundle and the FAST HUG mnemonic (feeding, analgesia, sedation, thromboprophylaxis, head of bed, stress-ulcer prophylaxis, glucose), plus lines, antibiotics, medications, skin and family. Ticks are for this page only: nothing is saved, and they clear when you leave or tap Clear.

In a cohort of 15,226 adults in 68 ICUs, days with the whole bundle done were followed by lower hospital death within 7 days (adjusted hazard ratio 0.32), less next-day ventilation, coma and delirium. It is an observational study: sicker patients are harder to wake and mobilize, so part of the effect may be who could receive the bundle.

1 · The bundle

Six letters, one patient awake and moving

Each letter makes the next one possible: no pain lets you lighten sedation, light sedation lets them breathe and move. Tap a letter.

2 · Wake up and breathe

Turn the sedation off, then see if they can breathe

A breathing trial on a patient who is deeply sedated tests the drug, not the lungs. Tap each step.

3 · Stress-ulcer prophylaxis

Fewer bleeds, same survival

Acid suppression lowers clinically important bleeding in at-risk patients, but no trial has shown that it saves lives. Switch between bleeding and death.

4 · Glucose

Not too high, and never chasing normal

Slide the glucose. Tight control (81 to 108) caused more deaths and far more severe lows than a conventional range.

5 · Feeding

Feed the gut early, and do not overfeed the first week

Slide the weight to see the first-week energy and protein ranges.

6 · Line days

Every device day is a day it can infect

The question is not whether a device was needed, but whether it is still needed today. Tap a device.
