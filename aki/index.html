KDIGO 2012 · definition and stage

Is it AKI, and how bad?

AKI is any one of: creatinine up 0.3 mg/dL or more within 48 hours; creatinine 1.5 times baseline or more, known or presumed to have happened within the prior 7 days; urine under 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours.

StageCreatinineUrine output
11.5–1.9 × baseline, or up ≥ 0.3 mg/dL< 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6–12 h
22.0–2.9 × baseline< 0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥ 12 h
33.0 × baseline, or ≥ 4.0 mg/dL, or dialysis started< 0.3 mL/kg/h for ≥ 24 h, or none for ≥ 12 h

Why: The stage is the worse of the two columns. A creatinine of 4.0 or more counts as stage 3 only once the definition above is met, so stable kidney disease is not AKI.

More

Acute kidney disease (KDIGO 2012): kidney function abnormal for less than 3 months, for example creatinine up more than 50% or GFR down 35% or more. AKD includes AKI; AKD without AKI is the slower form. The 2026 draft keeps the creatinine and urine thresholds, adds cystatin C and kidney damage biomarkers, and splits AKI that lasts 48 hours or less (transient) from longer (persistent).

Cystatin C can help when creatinine is unreliable (very low muscle mass, amputation, drugs that block creatinine secretion): KDIGO 2026 draft Rec 1.1.1 (2B).

Urine indices

FENa and FEUrea, and when they mislead

FENa = (urine Na × serum Cr) ÷ (serum Na × urine Cr) × 100

FEUrea = (urine urea N × serum Cr) ÷ (BUN × urine Cr) × 100

  • FENa under 1% fits a prerenal pattern; tubular injury and obstruction usually run higher (in oliguric patients, prerenal under 1, tubular necrosis over 3).
  • Best when oliguric with no kidney disease and no diuretic: sensitivity 95%, specificity 91%. On diuretics specificity falls to 54%.
  • FEUrea 35% or under fits a prerenal pattern; urea is reabsorbed passively, so diuretics move it less. One study (n = 102) found it better than FENa on diuretics; a second (n = 99) found specificity of 33% on diuretics.
  • FENa can be low despite intrinsic disease: acute glomerulonephritis, early obstruction, some sepsis.
  • FEUrea is unreliable in sepsis, osmotic diuresis, mannitol or acetazolamide.

Why: Both indices ask one question: is the tubule still able to hold on to what was filtered? A diuretic answers "no" on the tubule's behalf.

More

The KDIGO 2026 draft advises reserving FENa and FEUrea for selected situations because of their limited overall usefulness (PP 2.5.4). In cirrhosis, FENa is an adjunct; very low values favour hepatorenal AKI, but not on their own (PP 2.5.5).

What each finding points to

FindingPoints to
Muddy brown granular casts, tubular epithelial cellsAcute tubular injury
No casts, no tubular cells (bland)Prerenal or obstruction more likely; does not exclude interstitial nephritis
Red cell casts, dysmorphic red cellsGlomerular disease, usually
White cell casts, sterile pyuriaInterstitial nephritis; also pyelonephritis
Dipstick blood with few or no red cellsHeme pigment: myoglobin (check CK) or hemoglobin

Why: A cast forms inside a tubule and takes its shape, so whatever is caught in it came from the kidney itself.

More

The KDIGO 2026 draft suggests urine microscopy, especially when the cause is unclear (Rec 2.5.1, 2D). Automated urinalysis is not reliable enough to diagnose tubular injury, glomerulonephritis or interstitial nephritis. Urine eosinophils have limited value: neither necessary nor sufficient for interstitial nephritis (PP 2.5.2).

In a study of 267 patients, any casts or tubular cells strongly predicted tubular necrosis when it was suspected; when prerenal AKI was suspected, a sediment with neither had a negative predictive value of 91%. Red cell casts can occur in interstitial nephritis, and about 1 in 5 cases of interstitial nephritis have a bland sediment (review: Indian J Nephrol 2024, doi 10.25259/ijn_362_23).

Imaging

Bladder scan first; ultrasound when obstruction is possible

  • Bladder scan or catheter flush first whenever retention is possible.
  • Kidney ultrasound when there is anuria or oliguria, urinary symptoms, stones or past hydronephrosis, recurrent urinary infection, prostate disease, neurogenic bladder, pelvic cancer or surgery, a single kidney, or a catheter that drains poorly; and when the cause stays unclear.
  • Low yield when none of those clues is present and a prerenal, heart failure or drug cause is evident.

Why: Obstruction is fixable, but in hospitalized AKI it is uncommon; the clues pick out who has it.

More

In 797 inpatients with AKI who had ultrasound, 10.6% had hydronephrosis and 3.3% needed an intervention. In the study's low-risk group, 3.1% had hydronephrosis and 0.4% needed an intervention. That group was defined by a seven-factor rule that included race; this tool does not use race and does not claim that study's yield for its own advice.

Drugs

Stop, switch, reduce, monitor

  • Stop: NSAIDs. Hold while creatinine rises or the patient is dry or hypotensive: ACE inhibitor, ARB, ARNI, MRA, diuretics (unless overloaded), SGLT2 inhibitor.
  • Metformin: the label contraindicates it below eGFR 30, and says to stop it in shock, sepsis or other hypoxic states.
  • Avoid aminoglycosides when an alternative exists; if unavoidable, dose with pharmacy using levels. Lipid amphotericin over conventional.
  • Vancomycin for serious MRSA infection: dose by AUC (400–600), not trough alone.
  • Do not dose by an eGFR equation while creatinine is changing: follow the creatinine and urine output trend.
  • Raise creatinine without lowering GFR: trimethoprim, cimetidine, cobicistat, dolutegravir.

Why: Drugs are the most reversible cause, and every renally cleared dose is now too high.

More

The KDIGO 2026 draft recommends multimodal drug and nephrotoxin stewardship (Rec 4.2.1, 1C) and starting or resuming RAS inhibitors after AKI in adults unless contraindicated (Rec 6.2.1, 1B). Holding them during the rise is expert practice; the draft also suggests temporarily stopping some drug classes during volume-depleting illness (Rec 4.11.1, 2D). SGLT2 inhibitors and RAS inhibitors lower GFR through haemodynamics without injuring the kidney.

Iodinated contrast: do not delay a needed study; lowest dose; 0.9% saline for high-risk patients who are not overloaded (2026 draft Rec 4.9.2.1, 1B). Bicarbonate and acetylcysteine add nothing (PRESERVE). Metformin and contrast: the US label stops it at eGFR 30–60, the 2026 draft only with AKI or eGFR 30 or under.

Supportive care and dialysis

What helps, what does not, when to dialyse

  • Crystalloid, not colloid, for volume (KDIGO 2012, 2B; 2026 draft, 1B). Buffered crystalloid over 0.9% saline unless traumatic brain injury (2026 draft, 1B).
  • Vasopressors with fluids in vasodilatory shock (2012, 1C); MAP above 65 (2026 draft, 2C).
  • Diuretics for volume overload only, not to treat AKI (2012, 2C).
  • No low-dose dopamine (2012, 1A), fenoldopam (2C) or natriuretic peptide (2C to prevent, 2B to treat).
  • Dialysis urgently for life-threatening fluid, potassium or acid-base problems that do not respond to treatment, uremic complications (pericarditis, encephalopathy, bleeding), or a dialyzable poison.
  • Without those, starting early does not help: wait and watch (2026 draft Rec 5.1.1, 1C; STARRT-AKI).

Why: Most AKI recovers if perfusion is restored and the injury stops; dialysis replaces function, it does not speed recovery.

More

KDIGO 2012, 5.1.1 (not graded): start dialysis emergently when life-threatening changes in fluid, electrolyte and acid-base balance exist. Hepatorenal AKI: vasoconstrictor therapy (2026 draft Rec 3.6.1, 1C); see cirrhosis.

Workup · tap each result you have

Narrow the differential

Why: Urine and a bladder scan are cheap and fast; together they split the kidney into before, inside and after.

More

Send first: basic metabolic panel, urinalysis with microscopy on a fresh sample, spot urine sodium, creatinine and urea nitrogen (before diuretics if possible), a bladder scan, and a medicine review. CK if the patient was down, crushed, seized or is on a statin with pain.

Send after a result: kidney ultrasound if obstruction is possible or the cause is unclear. With red cell casts or blood and protein: nephrology the same day and serologies as they advise. Serum free light chains and protein electrophoresis if myeloma is possible.

Faded means less likely, never excluded: two causes often coexist (sepsis and contrast, heart failure and a diuretic).
