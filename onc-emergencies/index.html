1 · The first hour

Antibiotics before answers

Without neutrophils an infection gives few signs, and gram-negative bacteremia can kill within hours. Step through the clock.

The MASCC score

3 · Narrowing

Where is the infection?

First, for everyone: two blood culture sets (one from each line lumen plus one peripheral), CBC with differential, chemistry, liver tests, lactate; urine and chest imaging if there are symptoms. Then tap what you find: sources that no longer fit fade, each with the reason.

4 · TLS mechanism

Where uric acid comes from

Dying cells release nucleic acids, which the body breaks down to uric acid. The two drugs act at different steps. Tap one.

5 · TLS injury

Crystals in the tubule

Uric acid and calcium phosphate crystallize in slow, concentrated urine. Each fix has a trade-off. Tap one.

6 · TLS risk

Low, intermediate, high

Risk comes from the disease, its burden and the kidney. A kidney problem moves a patient up one step. Tap a class.

7 · Evidence

The studies behind it

For education only. Not a substitute for clinical judgment. For adults with cancer. Out of scope: children, hematopoietic cell transplant protocols, and product-specific TLS ramp-up schedules (venetoclax, CAR T-cell therapy, bispecific antibodies), which follow their own labels. Only cefepime is labeled in the US for empiric therapy of febrile neutropenia; the other beta-lactam doses are the labels' doses for other serious infections, and renal adjustment is in the empiric antibiotics tool. Febuxostat has no US indication for TLS. Formulary, antibiogram, rasburicase dosing policy (weight-based or fixed) and G6PD testing differ by hospital: adjust to your institution, and involve oncology, pharmacy and, for TLS, nephrology. Evidence tiers are shown as IDSA 2011 strength and quality (A–C, I–III), ASCO/IDSA 2018 strength, Cairo 2010 grade (A–D), BSH 2025 GRADE (1–2, A–D), the drug label, or "expert practice" for choices made for this tool.
