Every page

Three questions before you decide whether to go

  1. Vitals now, against this patient's baseline. Ask for a full set; a number alone is not a vital sign.
  2. Is it new, and why are they in hospital? Diagnosis, code status, and anything done in the last few hours (procedure, new drug, transfusion).
  3. What has already been done? Then decide: see now, see soon, or advise by phone and ask to be called back with a repeat.

Why: the nurse sees the patient and you do not; a short structured call turns a worry into a decision you can defend.

More

Go and see the patient for any abnormal vital sign that is new, any change in mental status, any new chest pain or shortness of breath, or whenever the nurse is worried. Close every phone call with what you want to be called back for ("call me if the pressure is under 90 again"). Before you hand back in the morning, write a short cross-cover note on anyone you saw.

expert practice

When to call for help

Call your senior or the rapid response team for any of these

  • Airway threatened
  • Respiratory rate under 5 or over 36, or respiratory arrest
  • Heart rate under 40 or over 140
  • Systolic pressure under 90
  • Sudden fall in level of consciousness (Glasgow Coma Scale down more than 2)
  • Repeated or prolonged seizures
  • Anyone seriously worried about the patient

Why: deterioration is usually visible in the vital signs hours before an arrest; calling early is the point of the system.

More

These are the calling criteria used in the MERIT trial (Hillman, Lancet 2005). Most hospitals add their own, such as oxygen saturation under 90% despite oxygen, new chest pain, or low urine output. Adjust to your institution: use your hospital's criteria when they differ.

MERIT itself (a cluster trial of 23 hospitals) did not show fewer arrests, unplanned ICU admissions or unexpected deaths with a rapid response team; it did show more emergency calls. Calling is still right: the criteria name a sick patient.

2 · Low pressure

Pressure = flow × resistance

Mean arterial pressure is cardiac output times vascular resistance. Each kind of shock drops one, and the body raises the other. Tap a type.

3 · Low oxygen

The oxygen curve has a cliff

Slide the oxygen tension. Above 60 mm Hg the curve is flat; below it, saturation falls fast.
