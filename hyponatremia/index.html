Diagnosis · in order

From sodium to cause

  1. Is it hypotonic? Glucose: add 2.4 to the sodium for every 100 mg/dL above 100. Measured osmolality under 275 is always hypotonic.
  2. Urine osmolality (spot): 100 or under means ADH is off. Excess water for the solute taken in: primary polydipsia, low solute intake (beer potomania, "tea and toast").
  3. Urine sodium (same sample), when urine osmolality is over 100: 30 or under means low effective circulating volume. Edema: heart failure, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome. No edema: vomiting or diarrhea, third spacing, diuretics used earlier.
  4. Urine sodium over 30: on a diuretic or with kidney disease, those explain it, but SIAD can coexist. Otherwise, by volume status. Low: vomiting, primary adrenal insufficiency, renal or cerebral salt wasting, hidden diuretics. Normal: SIAD, secondary adrenal insufficiency, severe hypothyroidism, hidden diuretics.

Why: Urine osmolality reads ADH; urine sodium reads what the kidney thinks of the circulating volume. Two numbers sort most cases before the volume exam, which is unreliable.

More

Not hypotonic (osmolality 275 or over): glucose, mannitol, glycine, contrast and other solutes that stay outside cells pull water out and dilute sodium. Very high lipids or protein (including IVIG and paraproteins) cause pseudohyponatremia, a measurement artefact in labs that dilute the sample. Urea and alcohols raise measured osmolality but cross cells freely, so a patient with kidney failure or intoxication can be hypotonic with a normal measured osmolality.

Do not measure vasopressin to diagnose SIAD. Urine sodium can be under 30 in SIAD with a very low salt intake.

European guideline 2014, 6.2–6.3 (1D–2D) and Table 10

SIAD

Diagnostic criteria

All of these:

  • Effective serum osmolality under 275 mOsm/kg
  • Urine osmolality over 100 mOsm/kg while osmolality is low
  • Clinically euvolemic
  • Urine sodium over 30 mmol/L on a normal salt and water intake
  • No adrenal, thyroid, pituitary or kidney failure
  • No recent diuretic

Why: SIAD is ADH without a reason, so every cause of a reasonable ADH (low volume, low cortisol, a diuretic) has to be ruled out first.

More

Supporting: uric acid under 4 mg/dL; serum urea under 3.6 mmol/L (BUN about 10 mg/dL); fractional excretion of sodium over 0.5%, of urea over 55%, of uric acid over 12%; no rise after isotonic saline; correction with fluid restriction.

Causes: cancers (lung, especially small cell; oropharynx; gut; genitourinary; lymphoma); lung disease (pneumonia, abscess, tuberculosis, asthma, positive-pressure ventilation); brain disease (meningitis, encephalitis, bleeding, stroke, tumor, trauma, Guillain–Barré, delirium tremens); nausea, pain, stress, anesthesia, endurance exercise.

Drugs: SSRIs, tricyclics, venlafaxine; carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, valproate, lamotrigine; antipsychotics; vincristine, platinum, cyclophosphamide, ifosfamide, methotrexate; opioids, MDMA, NSAIDs, proton pump inhibitors, amiodarone; desmopressin, oxytocin, terlipressin, vasopressin.

European guideline 2014, Tables 6 and 7

SIAD · calculator

Will fluid restriction work?

Ratio: enter all three

Why: When urine sodium plus potassium is above serum sodium, the urine carries no free water out, so restricting what goes in cannot raise the sodium.

More

Restriction is likely to fail with any of: urine osmolality over 500; urine Na + K above serum Na; 24-hour urine under 1500 mL; sodium rising less than 2 mmol/L a day over 24–48 h on 1 L a day or less.

Restriction means all fluids, not only water; restrict fluid, not salt. The EFFUSE-FLUID trial set the limit by this ratio (under 1000 or under 500 mL a day); a lower limit for a higher ratio. Restriction to 1 L a day raised sodium by a median 3 mmol/L at day 3, against 1 without it.

On a diuretic · calculator

Fractional excretion of urate

FEurate: enter all four (same units in urine and serum)

Why: A diuretic puts sodium in the urine whatever the volume, so urine sodium cannot separate SIAD from depletion. Urate excretion stays high in SIAD and falls with volume depletion, whether or not a diuretic is running.

More

FEurate = (urine urate × serum creatinine) ÷ (serum urate × urine creatinine) × 100. In 86 patients with sodium under 130, a cutoff of 12% identified SIAD with a positive predictive value of 100% in those on diuretics (area under the curve 0.96); urine sodium did worse (0.85).

Why: Urea adds solute so the kidney must carry out water with it; a loop diuretic blunts urine concentration; an SGLT2 inhibitor adds glucose as the solute; tolvaptan blocks ADH at the kidney.

More

Urea: no randomized trial. Across observational studies sodium rose by a mean 9.6 mmol/L; the usual side effect is the taste. It is not immune to overcorrection: recheck as for any treatment.

Tolvaptan (US label): start or restart only in hospital. 15 mg once daily; raise to 30 mg after at least 24 hours, to 60 mg at most. Avoid fluid restriction in the first 24 hours. No more than 30 days (liver injury). Avoid with liver disease, including cirrhosis. Contraindicated when hypovolemic, anuric, unable to sense or respond to thirst, on a strong CYP3A inhibitor, or with polycystic kidney disease outside its program. Not for patients who need sodium raised urgently. Boxed warning: too rapid a rise (over 12 mEq/L in 24 hours) can cause osmotic demyelination; slower rates may be advisable with severe malnutrition, alcoholism or advanced liver disease. Label indication: sodium under 125, or less marked but symptomatic and resistant to restriction.

Adjust to your institution: urea and tolvaptan availability differ by formulary.

Workup · tap each result you have

Narrow the differential

Why: Tonicity first, then urine osmolality for ADH, then urine sodium for volume. Each result removes a branch.

More

Send with the first draw: serum osmolality, glucose, creatinine, potassium, and a spot urine osmolality and sodium (with potassium) taken at the same time, before treatment.

Send after those are back: if urine osmolality is over 100 and urine sodium over 30 without a diuretic, a TSH and a morning cortisol before calling it SIAD. On a diuretic, uric acid in serum and urine with creatinine for FEurate. If osmolality is normal, lipids and total protein.

Faded means unlikely or less likely, never excluded: two causes often coexist (a thiazide in a patient with SIAD, vomiting on top of heart failure).
