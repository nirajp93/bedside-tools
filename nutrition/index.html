Prokinetics

Erythromycin first, for a day or two

  • Erythromycin IV 100 to 250 mg three times a day; use it for 24 to 48 hours and stop by day 3, when its effect has faded.
  • Metoclopramide 10 mg two to three times a day, alone or with erythromycin; lower the dose in kidney failure.
  • Both prolong the QT interval: check the ECG and the other QT drugs.
  • Still not tolerating after a prokinetic: a tube past the pylorus rather than stopping feeds.

Why: erythromycin acts on motilin receptors and empties the stomach, but the receptors stop responding within a few days.

QT drugs
More

ESPEN 2023: IV erythromycin first-line (grade B), metoclopramide alone or combined (grade 0). ASPEN 2016 gives metoclopramide 10 mg four times a day with renal adjustment and notes QT prolongation for both.

Aspiration

Head up, continuous feeds

  • Head of the bed at 30 to 45 degrees.
  • Continuous rather than bolus feeding in the ICU.
  • A tube past the pylorus for patients at high risk of aspiration.
